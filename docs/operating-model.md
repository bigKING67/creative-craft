# Operating model

## Roles

One person or agent may perform several roles, but decisions remain distinct.

- **Owner:** business objective, budget, final approval.
- **Strategist:** audience, tension, insight, proposition, measurement.
- **Creative lead:** routes, selection, creative coherence.
- **Art director:** visual language and image direction.
- **Director/editor:** narrative, performance, camera, motion, sound, timing.
- **Prompt/operator:** provider execution and generation receipts.
- **Reviewer:** output inspection and evidence-aware evaluation.
- **Producer:** schedule, asset readiness, rights, delivery.
- **Analyst:** test design and performance learning.

## Stage gates

### G0 — rights and source readiness

Can references, people, brands, claims, music, and source assets be used?

### G1 — brief readiness

Is the communication task specific enough to reject irrelevant work?

### G2 — route readiness

Are routes distinct, feasible, and comparable?

### G3 — direction lock

Are message, visual, narrative, sound, references, and invariants explicit?

### G4 — production job readiness

Does the job pass schema/provider validation and preserve required truth?

### G5 — output inspection

Was the actual output reviewed against the job and brief?

### G6 — adaptation readiness

Is the concept locked, and are channel-specific changes explicit?

### G7 — delivery readiness

Do files, rights, specifications, approvals, and manifests agree?

## Small-task shortcut

A one-image edit may compress G1–G3 into:

- intended use;
- exact change;
- exact preserve list;
- output specification;
- source rights.

The shortcut does not remove output inspection or delivery honesty.

## Learning loop

A finding becomes reusable only when context, variable, evidence, and scope are
known. Store:

- what changed;
- where;
- for whom;
- measured effect;
- confidence;
- alternative explanations;
- whether it repeated.

Do not add “always use X” rules from one successful asset.
